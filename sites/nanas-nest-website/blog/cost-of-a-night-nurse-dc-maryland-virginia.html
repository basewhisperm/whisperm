How Much Does a Newborn Night Nurse Cost in DC, Maryland & Virginia?

Nana's Nest Team · Planning & Budgeting

It's one of the first questions every family asks, and one of the hardest to answer with a single number — because night nursing rates in the DC metro area depend on several real variables. Here's how to think about it.

What Affects the Rate

  • Credentials — a Registered Nurse typically commands a higher rate than a non-RN newborn care specialist
  • Hours & nights per week — a single occasional night costs more per hour than an ongoing, multi-night weekly schedule
  • Single baby vs. multiples — twins and triplets are generally billed at a premium
  • Short-term vs. ongoing — intensive newborn-week coverage is priced differently than a longer, part-time arrangement

General Ranges in the DMV

As a general guide, families in the Washington DC, Maryland, and Northern Virginia area can expect credentialed overnight newborn care to fall roughly in the mid-double-digit hourly range, with RN-level care and multiples typically priced toward the higher end. Every family's quote from Nana's Nest is personalized to your household, schedule, and caregiver match — request a private consultation for an exact quote.

Ways to Budget

  • A short, intensive 1–2 week block right after delivery, when needs are highest
  • Fewer nights per week stretched over a longer period
  • Combining night nursing with a later sleep training package instead of open-ended nightly coverage

Is It Worth It?

Sleep isn't a luxury in the postpartum period — it's tied directly to physical recovery, mental health, and a parent's ability to safely function during the day. Many families tell us the investment paid for itself within the first week, simply in how much more capable and present they felt.
