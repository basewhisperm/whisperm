Night Nurse vs. Postpartum Doula: What's the Difference?

Nana's Nest Team · Newborn Care

"Night nurse" and "postpartum doula" get used almost interchangeably by exhausted new parents — but the two roles are genuinely different, and knowing the distinction helps you book the right support the first time.

Quick Definitions

A night nurse (also called a newborn care specialist or baby nurse) focuses almost entirely on hands-on infant care overnight: feedings, soothing, diapering, and sleep, so parents can rest through the night. Despite the name, "night nurse" doesn't always mean a licensed nurse — at Nana's Nest, ours are RNs or certified Newborn Care Specialists.

A postpartum doula supports the whole postpartum period — a parent's physical recovery, emotional adjustment, feeding education, and light household support — and can work day or night shifts.

What a Night Nurse Focuses On

  • Overnight feedings and burping
  • Diaper changes and soothing
  • Sleep tracking and gentle sleep coaching from birth
  • A calm morning handoff

What a Postpartum Doula Focuses On

  • Physical recovery support (rest, nutrition, comfort)
  • Breastfeeding or bottle-feeding education
  • Emotional support and normalizing the adjustment period
  • Light meal prep or household help alongside baby care

Side by Side

Night NursePostpartum Doula
Primary focusOvernight infant careWhole-family recovery & support
Typical hoursOvernight onlyDay or night
Best forUninterrupted parental sleepRecovery, feeding & emotional support

Which One Is Right for Your Family?

If your top priority is sleep, start with a night nurse. If you want broader recovery and feeding support alongside baby care, a postpartum doula may be the better first call. Many families ultimately want both at different points in the fourth trimester — which is exactly why Nana's Nest matches families with the right caregiver for where they are, not a fixed package.
